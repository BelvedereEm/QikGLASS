How the head works

The head is five printed parts plus the QWORK cutter head. There's nothing else: no rod, bearing, metal spring, screw or clip.

  • Body: sits in the spindle clamp. Its long bushing at the bottom keeps the shaft upright.
  • Shaft: drops in from the top. A flange stops it on the body's inner lip, so nothing has to hold it in. Its domed top is the swivel: only the centre of the dome carries the load, so it spins almost freely even under 3–5 kg, and that free spin lets the wheel steer. The split collet that holds the cutter is printed on its bottom end.
  • Spring: a printed tube with staggered slots, whose rings bend like a stack of leaf springs. Its solid foot sits on the shaft's dome. It comes in three strengths (below).
  • Cap: screws onto the top of the body and presses on the spring. This is the pressure adjustment. Each of its 12 grip flutes is 1/12 turn, or 0.25 mm.
  • Nut: screws up the collet and grips the QWORK head, which sits 1.5 mm off-centre. The wheel trails behind the swivel like a shopping-cart caster, and the G-code page shifts every path by that offset.

Measure two things first

Measure both with calipers, enter them at the top of qikglass_scoring_head.scad, then export the STLs from free OpenSCAD. The included STLs use the defaults shown.

Clamp bore

Take the spindle out and measure the motor where the clamp grips it. A bare 775 is about 42 mm. Across its steel flux ring it's about 45.5 mm. Only the body changes.

clamp_d = 42.0

Cutter-head shank

Loosen the small screw on the handle, pull a head out, and measure the round part that sat inside the handle. Only the shaft changes.

head_shank_d = 7.0

Spring strength

Print the medium spring first. These figures are estimates for PETG. Your printer and filament will shift them, so always check with the kitchen scale (see Calibrate & score).

PETG for everything is best, and the spring must be PETG, because PLA creeps and loses force. Use 0.2 mm layers and 4 walls, 40% infill unless noted. Fits are set by bush_clr, slide_clr and thread_clr in the CAD file. If the shaft binds, raise bush_clr by 0.1. If the wheel wobbles, lower it.

Assembly

  1. Drop the shaft into the top of the body, collet end first, so it slides down through the bushing until its flange sits on the inner lip.
  2. Drop the spring in on top, foot first. Its foot sits on the shaft's dome.
  3. Screw the cap on until it just touches the spring. You'll take it off again to fit the head into the machine.
  4. Push the QWORK head's shank up into the collet under the body. Turn it so the wheel's plane lines up with the two grooves on the shaft, then screw the nut up the collet until the head is gripped. If the wheel is turned sideways, it won't steer.
  5. Push the collet up and let go: it should spring back. Spin it: it should turn freely. A drop of oil on the dome and in the bushing helps.

Swapping spindle ⇄ glass head

  1. Power down and unplug the spindle lead at the board.
  2. Loosen the clamp and take the spindle out. Unscrew the cap (the spring can stay inside the body). Push the body up through the clamp from below until the threaded top sticks out above it, tighten the clamp, then screw the cap back on to your pressure setting.
  3. Re-zero Z on the glass every time (see Calibrate & score).
  4. Going back: reverse this, plug the spindle in, and re-zero Z with a bit.

Never run a milling file with the glass head fitted. Router files dive several millimetres deep and will bottom out the spring and break glass. Keep glass files in their own folder on the SD card.

Your machine: connecting and checking settings

This is from the CNC3-3018PRO manual and USB stick that came in the box. Its board is a Woodpecker v3.4 running GRBL 1.1f.

  1. Driver: install Software for Control the machine(3Axis)\Driver\CH340SER.EXE once. The machine then shows up in Device Manager under Ports as USB-SERIAL CH340 (COMx).
  2. Candle: run Grblcontrol (Candle).exe from the same folder. In Settings, pick that COM port at 115200 baud. The console shows Grbl 1.1f ['$' for help] when it's connected. Unplug the offline controller while you use the USB cable.
  3. Check the speed limits: type $$ in Candle's console. Factory values are $110=1000, $111=1000 (X and Y top speed, mm/min), $112=800 and $120–$122=30 (acceleration). The G-code page's default score feed of 900 stays under that. Leave these settings alone until the head is working.
  4. Zeroing: Candle's zero XY and zero Z buttons do the same as G10 L20 P1 X0 Y0 and Z0 in this guide. Jog with the arrows, using 0.1 or 0.01 mm steps near the glass.
  5. Offline controller: it reads .nc files, which the Save button makes. Plug the controller into a PC with its USB cable to copy files onto it, like a card reader. Then plug it into the board with the PC unplugged.
  6. No limit switches: the board has ports for them, but homing is off ($22=0). Use the zero pointer or park-and-power-off to find X0 Y0, as described below.

The spindle clamp on this kit is a split plastic ring tightened by one bolt, and the manual warns not to overtighten it. Snug is enough for the printed body. Measure the ring's inside diameter for clamp_d before printing.

Put every blank in the same place

The G-code always puts X0 Y0 at the blank's front-left corner, with the 6 in side running along X. Two things keep that repeatable: a jig that doesn't move, and a quick zeroing routine each time the machine is switched on.

Fit the jig once

  1. The jig bolts straight onto the 3018's aluminium bed. No spoilboard is needed. The bed's T-slots run left to right (along X), 44 mm apart. You need six M6 bolts with T-nuts, the same size as the bed clamps (5.8 mm thread), 10–12 mm long, with washers.
  2. Print qikglass_jig.stl and qikglass_padstop.stl. The jig is an L: the front rail runs along X and the left rail along Y. The pad stop is a small separate block that holds a 30 × 20 mm scrap of the same glass for the wheel-alignment pad.
  3. Slide T-nuts into the front slot and bolt the front rail's three holes into that one slot. That lines the rail up with X. The left rail's two holes then land in the next two slots back, 44 mm apart (marked "44"). Keep the corner near the front-left of the bed, with at least 20 mm of travel left below X0 and Y0.
  4. Push the pad stop against the end of the front rail and bolt it into the same slot. Its marked centre is X179.4 Y10, the G-code page's default Pad X and Pad Y.
  5. Glue 2 mm felt or cork onto the bed inside the L, covering the glass area and the pad. Set felt_t in the CAD file to your felt's thickness before printing. The rails then stand 1.5 mm above the felt: high enough to stop the glass, low enough that the wheel never touches them.
  6. Check it's square: fit the zero pointer (below), lower it just above the front rail's inside edge, and jog along X. The tip should follow the edge. If it drifts, loosen the bolts, nudge the rail and retighten.

The bolt heads sit 14 mm outside the glass, clear of the cutter's fork and nut. If your slots are a different distance apart, change slot_pitch in the CAD file. If your bolts are a different size, change bolt_d.

Zero X and Y

The 3018 doesn't know where it is when it's switched on, so X0 Y0 has to be set at the jig corner. There are two ways to do it.

  1. Zero pointer (any time, most accurate): unscrew the collet nut, take the QWORK head out and slide qikglass_pointer.stl up over the bottom of the shaft. Its tip sits exactly on the pivot axis, which is the point the G-code moves. Jog the tip into the jig's inside corner, just above the felt, and press the controller's XY zero (or send G10 L20 P1 X0 Y0). Take the pointer off, refit the cutter and nut, and zero Z on the glass as usual.
  2. Park and power off (quick): every QikGLASS file ends with the head parked at X0 Y0. Switch the machine off there, don't move the gantry by hand, and after switching on just press XY zero. Check it with the pointer every few sessions, or after anything bumps the machine.

If your 3018 has limit switches, you can turn on homing ($22=1) and save the jig corner as a permanent work offset with G10 L2 P1. Then $H at power-up restores it with no touching off. This is optional. The routine above works without switches.

Every blank

  • Brush the felt clean, then push the blank firmly into the jig corner, 6 in side along the front rail. The corner notch leaves room for a chipped corner.
  • Keep the scrap pad strip against its stop. Replace it when it's covered in scratches.
  • Any rectangle works in the jig, including the leftover strip from an earlier job. Set Glass blank to “Custom size” and type its width (along the front rail) and depth in inches or mm. After a job that saves a strip, “Use as next blank” fills the size in for you, and the page remembers it until next time. Glass wider than 160 mm hits the pad stop. Unbolt it for wider glass.
  • Zero Z on the glass surface (paper pinch), then run the file.

Set the pressure with a kitchen scale

  1. Screw the cap down until it just touches the spring, then a quarter turn more (3 flutes) so the spring has a little preload.
  2. Put the scale on the bed under the wheel and tare it.
  3. Jog Z down in 0.1 mm steps until the scale first jumps. Call that height "touch".
  4. Keep stepping down until the scale reads about 3 kg. The distance below "touch" is your Score depth. With the medium spring, expect about 3 mm.
  5. Run a test on scrap. A good score is a thin, even, barely visible line that makes a steady hiss. White flakes or a gritty crunch mean too much pressure. Skips or a line that won't break mean too little. Adjust in 0.5 kg steps. Most stained glass lands at 3–5 kg.
  6. For quick changes later, leave Score depth alone and turn the cap. Each flute adds about 0.25 mm, which is roughly 0.2 kg with the medium spring.

If the scale stops climbing as you go deeper, the spring has gone solid: print the firm one. Printed springs relax a little over the first few sessions, so recheck with the scale after the first week.

Zero Z on the glass for every sheet

Lay a strip of paper on the glass under the wheel and jog down until the paper is just pinched. Set Z0 there. The program then drives to Z −(score depth) and the spring supplies the force.

Draw scores the way glass breaks

  • Draw each score as one open line in Inkscape (document in mm, page = your sheet). Draw only score lines. Don't draw the sheet outline.
  • Each score runs from edge to edge, or ends where it meets an earlier score. Glass can't break along a line that stops in the middle of the sheet.
  • Never cross or retrace a score. The generator warns you if two lines cross.
  • Keep curves gentle. Deep inside curves need extra relief scores, as with hand cutting.
  • Break in the numbered order shown in the preview, running each score before the next one that ends on it.

Pieces, 6 × 4 blanks and relief scores

  • Every job goes on a 6 × 4 in blank (152.4 × 101.6 mm). Push the blank into the jig corner so its front-left corner is X0 Y0.
  • With Layout set to “Pack bottom-left”, pieces fill a row from left to right starting at the front-left corner, then the next row up, with a gap between them. Pieces are turned 90° when that keeps the rows low. Turn this off for streaky glass with a grain. Any pieces that don't fit move onto blank 2, 3, and so on, each with its own G-code file.
  • The unused glass ends up as one strip along the back. The trim score T runs across the blank above the top row: break it first and keep the strip for another project. Relief scores stop at the trim line, so the strip stays clean.
  • A closed outline is treated as a piece, with everything outside it as waste. With Relief scores set to Auto, the page adds relief lines out to the glass edge: a pinwheel of tangent lines around circles and outside curves, extensions of each sharp outside corner, and a comb of parallel lines out of inside curves such as a semicircle bite.
  • Relief lines (purple, marked R) are scored after the outline and end on it without crossing it. Break them first, starting with the ones nearest the glass edge, using running pliers or grozers. Then run the piece outline.
  • Sharp corners are split into separate sides, because a swivel wheel can't turn on the spot. Sharp inside corners are flagged. Score up to them from both sides and finish with a grinder.
  • Cut neighbouring pieces from separate blanks. Two outlines a line-width apart leave a sliver that won't break, and the page warns you when that happens.

Oil and running

  • The head has no reservoir once it's out of the handle. Touch a drop of cutting oil to the wheel before each sheet, or brush a thin film along the lines. Use the "Pause to re-oil" option for big sheets. It uses M0. If your offline controller ignores M0, run from a PC with Candle or UGS instead.
  • Files contain M5 and never turn the spindle on, but keep it unplugged anyway.
  • Wear safety glasses. Handle sheets with cut-resistant gloves, and brush chips off the felt between sheets so the glass sits flat.
